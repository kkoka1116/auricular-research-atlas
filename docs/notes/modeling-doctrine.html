Auricular stock and reconstruction modeling doctrine

Version 3 · 18 September 2026 · exploratory computational research

What the evidence changes

The natural cartilage junction can be part of the carving blank. A framework need not be carved from one individually labeled rib. Conversely, two adjacent labels are not automatically one sound piece of tissue.

Li et al. describe sixth–seventh base stock, eighth-cartilage helix stock, and antihelical stock from seventh/eighth remnants or the ninth. Their classification distinguishes an intact junction from separate cartilage requiring fixation, and also includes direct antihelical carving on a sufficiently thick base. These are alternative constructions, not evidence that our present four solids reproduce a validated surgical design. Li et al., 2021.

Firmin and Marchac describe a base spanning adjacent cartilage joined by a synchondrosis; their template figure specifically mentions the fifth–sixth junction. Firmin and Marchac, 2011. Anghinoni et al. describe a sixth–seventh block incorporating the synchondrosis. Anghinoni et al., 2015.

The user's International Dissection Course Handbook 2025, PDF page 6 (printed page 5), illustrates numbered rib simulators, a thinned curved helix strip, carved support pieces, and assembly. It supplies the construction concept, not measured patient cut coordinates, cartilage material properties, or a calibrated native-junction map. Its cited appendix template is absent from the supplied nine-page file.

Rules implemented in this iteration

  1. Preserve the native CT affine and millimeters. Keep the 58.9 mm reference benchmark and all current component STL dimensions fixed. This height is a population-based working benchmark, not a measured contralateral ear for these patients.
  2. Treat the fifth–sixth and sixth–seventh junctions and a larger sixth–seventh–eighth harvest envelope as candidate base sources, alongside isolated sixth and seventh baselines. The larger envelope is a search hypothesis; a fit is not proof of a recommended harvest.
  3. Form each candidate domain from the exact union of its labeled voxel cuboids. Never use a convex hull, dilation, closing, or gap filling to create extra donor tissue.
  4. Split the union into face-connected components. One continuous rigid cut piece must be certified inside one such component. Face connectivity and shared-face area describe the segmentation only; neither proves a mechanically intact synchondrosis.
  5. Identify whether each successful placement actually crosses labels, and recheck the same pose against each single label. A placement wholly in one rib is not credited as a multi-rib benefit.
  6. Distinguish three constructions: a native connected blank; a surgically fastened blank made from separate stock; and separately carved units assembled into an ear. This version certifies only native cut-stock containment. It does not credit a virtual bridge or an unmodeled fastening step as tissue.
  7. Search the seventh, eighth and ninth cartilage for the current forked antihelix. Search sixth–seventh stock for the tragal complex. The latter is a remnant only after joint allocation proves adequate separation from every other selected cut.
  8. Do not reuse tissue. Choices from overlapping label unions compete for the same physical stock. Whole-solid cover separation checks the selected cuts, including a helix if an eighth-cartilage region also supplies a base.
  9. Preserve unsuccessful cases. A bounded search without a certificate is not an impossibility proof. A failed current rigid solid may motivate a different carved architecture or a bending preform; it does not imply surgeons cannot reconstruct an ear.
  10. Keep the earlier rod experiment's limited scope: it illustrates bending of a selected native curved helix strip. It does not validate base deformation, fork mechanics, fixation, tissue quality, machining access, or clinical safety.

What is still a design decision

The current contoured base and Y-shaped ridge follow a skin-surface reference and may demand more out-of-plane depth than a clinically carved support. No flattening or shrinking is hidden inside this search. A future version can explicitly compare a shallower base, an integrated base/antihelix, and a divided/fastened base while preserving the intended final ear dimensions and defining connections. Those alternatives need surgeon-reviewed landmarks and physical validation.

Version 2 addition: expanded cadaveric inventory

The researcher explicitly authorized use of substantially more donor cartilage than a conventional autologous harvest. Search all available costal-cartilage labels on both sides, alongside the conventional-region comparison. Do not impose an unrequested cap of one side or ribs 6–9. Retain complete native connected components; one continuous carved unit cannot span a gap. Separate stock can supply separate units, and a later explicit assembly model can combine units. A fixed-length, fixed-section helix precursor may be explored in other ribs, but its source association is an engineering extension rather than a reported Nagata harvest choice. A new native rest curve has geometric containment only until its bending response is simulated.

This expands the inventory, not the CT segmentation boundary: there is no license to invent unscanned posterior cartilage, treat costal bone/sternum as cartilage, or infer usable material from an older donor. Patient masks are anatomy proxies, not a tissue-bank stock list. Donor age, processing and calcification remain separate from geometric availability.

Why a surgical junction may not be one continuous cartilage mask

A primary series of 733 grafts distinguished direct cartilage fusion (18.7%), synovial joints (74.2%) and loose connections (7.1%). A preserved surgical block can therefore include a joint and supporting tissues rather than homogeneous fused cartilage. Our face-connected cartilage-only criterion is deliberately narrower than that surgical definition. A missing mask bridge is not proof that a surgeon cannot harvest and stabilize the region. Kim et al., 2021.

A separate dissection study examined 28 cadavers bilaterally and found interchondral joints most commonly at sixth–seventh and seventh–eighth levels. This supports inspecting connected regions on both sides, while distinguishing joint anatomy from a solid carving blank. Akyeampong et al., 2024.

Verification refinement

The broad screen covers each current watertight solid with 0.5 mm cubes and uses a conservative native-grid distance-transform bound. Every original triangle's bounding cells and the enclosed interior are included. A loose cover or distance bound can reject an actually feasible pose.

For retained near-fit poses (search loss ≤10), a second verifier subdivides the original triangles, builds a 0.3 mm outer cover, computes distance to native background voxel cuboids independently, and refines uncertain cells up to four times. Children proven outside the original solid are discarded; all possible solid-containing children remain covered. The acceptance margin remains 0.8 mm. Only a complete cover pass is accepted. This improves verification at a fixed pose; it is not a geometric relaxation or a new surgical safety criterion. Original broad-search records remain available alongside refined records.

The finite search uses four locally optimized starts per part/domain, deterministic PCA-based initial poses and up to twelve pose checks. Each native connected component of sufficient volume is considered. This is a development search, not proof of completeness or a locked clinical study. Near-fit refinement and expanded inventory were development iterations; their results cannot be reported as held-out performance.

Comparator and inventory interpretation

Both inventory modes permit either side of the same case. The conventional-region column is a geometric starting-region comparator, not a complete simulation of living-donor harvest practice. In particular, eighth-only helix stock is a simple baseline; published techniques also use other sources and combinations. Improvement over that baseline must not be presented as a clinical advantage of cadaveric over autologous reconstruction.

The current release has 165 primary-center case IDs and 30 external IDs as standalone labels, whereas the dataset card describes 22 OOD cases. Both ZIPs contain 22 external label entries; their geometry differs from corresponding standalone versions. Preserve that release discrepancy and analyze alternate masks separately. Do not silently equate the 195 released identifiers with the paper's patient count or treat filename suffixes as validated ages.

The native connected-component screen ignores islands smaller than 100 voxels. For this intake, even 99 of the largest voxels amount to approximately 107.4 mm³, below the smallest current rigid part's 335.7 mm³ volume. Thus those islands cannot supply a whole current rigid piece. Runtime records reflect parallel development runs and are not controlled runtime comparisons.

Version 3 addition: cut shape and assembled shape may differ

Rigid treatment of the base, antihelix and tragal complex is a baseline modeling assumption, not a general surgical restriction. Published techniques include bending the antihelix to form an antitragus and securing it to the base. Wang et al., 2025.

The forming lab searches volume-preserving starting shapes and separately solves their conditional elastic response under temporary guides and tension-only attachments. Preserve final target size, account for compression and extension, distinguish geometric containment from strain estimates, and distinguish guide forces from stitch tensions. Wider strain screens are research sensitivities, not validated safe deformation limits. A cut that fits cannot be credited as an assembled-ear success without holding, contact and material validation.

Keep a matched rigid-search control. More optimization can recover a previously missed rigid placement; do not attribute such gains to bending. The first ten-adult experiment added rigid matches but no verified case/component fits attributable to the four deformation modes. Its demonstration preforms remain visibly unverified where stock containment failed. See inverse-forming methods and evidence.

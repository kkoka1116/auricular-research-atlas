Data, attribution and publication

This is the public edition of Krishna Koka’s Auricular Research Atlas, prepared 19 September 2026. It consolidates the completed development studies without rerunning or changing their numerical experiments.

What is included

The atlas contains the research narrative, sizing decisions, surgical evidence notes, component assembly, helix mechanics, forming and suture simulations, historical comparisons and verification records. Numerical data and model downloads are losslessly compressed for delivery. The browser restores the original asset formats when a download is clicked.

The public edition includes the synthetic validation viewer, ear-component assembly and forming animations, a reference-ear sizing comparison, numerical helix-mechanics charts and aggregate connected-stock results. Native cartilage geometry, CT previews, scan-coordinate placements and course-handbook images remain in the private research archive. Historical anatomy viewers are represented by summaries and research notes.

The construction animation is prescribed geometry. The mechanics animations show independently solved equilibrium states. Neither is a clinical instruction or a prediction of forming speed. The 58.9 mm target is a declared clinical-cohort benchmark. The original ear STL’s intrinsic physical units were not established.

Published external-ear reference

The external-ear geometry is adapted from Alberto Díez-Montiel, Alicia Pose-Díez-de-la-Lastra, Alba González-Álvarez, José Ignacio Salmerón, Javier Pascau and Santiago Ochandiano (2024), Tablet-based Augmented Reality and 3D Printed Templates in fully guided Microtia Reconstruction: A clinical workflow – Supplementary Material.

Source: Zenodo record 10958624. Related paper: 10.1186/s41205-024-00213-2. License: Creative Commons Attribution–ShareAlike 4.0.

Modifications include extraction of the intact ear component, orientation, declared height normalization, display simplification, manually drafted component geometry, and simulated deformation. No original CT volumes or NIfTI label files are bundled with this website.

These are patient CT anatomical proxies, not actual tissue-bank inventory. Ten G3 cases establish an adult category; individual ages, older-donor representativeness, tissue processing and material integrity are not verified. Case identifiers and alternate mask versions are not automatically independent donors.

User-supplied course handbook

The ASPO/AAFPRS International Virtual Dissection Course Handbook, Microtia Carving page, informed the construction roles and assembly sequence. It remains a user-supplied training reference; its exact stitch locations and tissue properties were not encoded. The handbook image is not republished in this public edition; its concepts are summarized in original prose. No full journal articles, surgical videos or commercial teaching materials are included.

Reproducibility and interpretation

The private research repository contains the computational source, configuration files, research notes and website build instructions. Repository access is separate from access to this public website. The website’s asset manifest records publication hashes, sizes and lossless compression, with original source hashes for copied assets. Publication copies redact local workstation paths. Earlier study reports retain their original dates, component architectures and validation counts; the current numerical suite passed 76 tests.

The current forming study found base 0/10, antihelix 3/10 and tragal 7/10 cut matches, with zero additional case–part fits attributable to the tested deformation modes beyond the updated rigid control. Its animated starting cuts are explicitly unverified. These new cuts have not been jointly allocated into a complete framework.

This is an evolving research prototype, not an established clinical or manufacturing protocol. Surgical review and independent physical validation remain required to interpret tissue behavior and clinical relevance.

The surgical supplement was downloaded and its published MD5 verified. Its ear-only reference was parsed successfully (104,713 triangles; open surface). The intake manifest records the original download, checksums and archive inventory. Do not assign millimetres to an STL solely because its coordinate magnitudes look plausible: STL itself does not encode physical units. Validate scale using source documentation or known measurements and record any unresolved assumption.

Proposed next milestone

  1. Select one documented external-ear reference. Preserve the original; inspect scale, orientation, holes and resolution. Annotate the anatomical regions used in measurement.
  2. Draft separate editable base, helix, antihelix and tragus/antitragus components. Define the back surfaces, thickness and joints explicitly; a skin-surface scan does not supply those details. Have Dr. Ritter review component boundaries and assembly requirements before treating them as surgical targets.
  3. Fix the intended assembled dimensions before matching. Future recipient-specific planning can use the unaffected ear; present public-data experiments use explicitly labeled reference sizes. Never resize during a placement search to manufacture a successful result.
  4. Match component stock requirements to CCSeg regions, allowing multiple components from a cartilage region while forbidding overlapping stock allocations. Confirm the provisional label identities. Preserve actual junctions and gaps; do not turn a disconnected union into an artificial solid blank.
  5. Distinguish the harvested/carved stock shape from the assembled shape when bending or splicing is intended. Those operations need explicit limits and physical validation. Rib-to-component assignment varies by technique; avoid treating one article's allocation as universal.
  6. Show a color-coded source-to-component map, assembled ear, dimensions, unallocated pieces and independently checked clearance. Keep en-bloc and assembled-framework experiments separate. Lock the method only after development and surgical review, then run the independent pilot in the original plan.

The current synthetic whole-shape result does not answer whether a collection of cartilage segments can supply a reconstructed ear. The existing importer, geometry measurements and containment verifier remain useful infrastructure for this next experiment.

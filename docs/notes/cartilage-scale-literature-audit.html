Cartilage scale and literature comparison

17 September 2026. Question: are the CCSeg cartilage models correctly scaled, and have their dimensions been checked against surgical literature?

The native millimetre scale is preserved. Newly measured long-axis spans are broadly consistent with published cartilage lengths. This supports gross size plausibility; it does not validate local usable thickness, anatomical completeness, clinical component fit or suitability as adult cadaveric stock. No anatomy was resized and no matching results were changed.

What had already been checked

The earlier framework-sizing audit checked CT units, physical-coordinate transforms and six native-mask/export comparisons. Pool verification subsequently checked all exported model extents. Those were conversion checks, not an anatomical comparison against literature.

Fresh independent audit

scripts/audit_cartilage_scale.py rereads all ten original label volumes independently of the production intake and meshing functions. It checks explicit mm units, voxel spacing, agreement of qform/sform coordinate transforms, original input hashes, and the physical bounds of every exported cartilage for labels 5–9 and 15–19 (100 exports). Native bounds are obtained from occupied voxel-index extrema expanded by half a voxel, transformed through the original affine. Export bounds agree within 0.001 mm; the maximum observed difference is 0.00000763 mm. This very small difference is numerical conversion agreement, not scan or segmentation accuracy.

All ten volumes have 0.5 mm spacing between slice centers. In-plane spacing ranges from 0.496094 to 0.716797 mm. Slice spacing is not slice thickness or guaranteed spatial resolution. The CCSeg source preprint, section 4.2 describes 1 mm slice thickness with overlapping 0.5/0.6 mm intervals for the primary scanners, consistent with this distinction.

For an independent size check, occupied voxel centers were transformed to mm and projected onto their first principal axis. Their maximum-minus-minimum projection is an automatic straight-axis span, not a clinician's endpoint length, curved centerline length, or usable graft length. Small disconnected regions were retained, as in the existing report. Native measurements reproduce the saved spans.

Comparison with primary surgical studies

Zanje et al., 2021 studied 35 microtia patients, average age 16.5 years. Eighth-cartilage mean lengths were 88.6 mm right and 90.5 mm left; ninth-cartilage means were 63.2 and 58.2 mm. Their method measured anatomical endpoints on coronal 3D views displaying the cartilage length. Our eighth-cartilage mean span of 89.7 mm is reassuring for overall scale. Our ninth-cartilage mean of 71.5 mm is higher; the cohorts and methods are different, and this is not an agreement study or a normative acceptance interval.

Sun et al., 2017 studied 37 six-year-old microtia patients. Reported right-cartilage length ranges were 61.1–94.9 mm (sixth), 84.5–119.1 mm (seventh), and 47.3–118.5 mm (eighth). These overlap our spans, supporting similar gross scale without establishing age-matched normality.

What remains unresolved

  • The source dataset includes children, adolescents, adults and a previously harvested group (CCSeg preprint, table 1). We have not established per-file age or harvest-group membership. Filename patterns are not verified demographics. These ten cases are not a validated adult donor inventory.
  • Automatic whole-object spans and bounding-box depths cannot substitute for local cross-sectional thickness, usable width or the geometry of the sixth–seventh junction. Curvature contributes to a bounding-box depth.
  • Matching the published measurement requires reviewed anatomical endpoints and equivalent measurement definitions. Scanner calibration and segmentation accuracy require evidence beyond a header/export comparison.
  • Our 61.5 mm ear is an adult population benchmark, not the matched opposite ear from any of these CT cases.

The appropriate next anatomical validation is to review rib identities and endpoints, measure local cross-sections and the sixth–seventh junction, and identify age/harvest groups. Preserve the native CT scale rather than resizing real anatomy to a population mean or to obtain a successful fit.

Reproduce

Run .venv/bin/python scripts/audit_cartilage_scale.py from the project root. Per-case headers, per-cartilage measurements, input/export hashes and summary statistics are saved in output/cartilage-scale-audit/audit.json. The existing anatomy and placement reports remain unchanged.

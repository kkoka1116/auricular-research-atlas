Methods and interpretation

Scope and geometry

The reference is an editable synthetic height-field solid with an ear-shaped outline, an outer relief ridge and an inner ridge. A polar triangulation joins paired top and bottom surfaces around a closed boundary. The default result has 1,794 vertices and 3,584 faces. It is intended to exercise numerical methods, not reproduce surgical framework architecture.

The nominal height is 60 mm. All coordinates and reported distances use millimetres. The resulting outline width differs from the nominal ellipse width, and is measured explicitly. +Y is labelled superior, +Z is relief; these are synthetic conventions, not patient coordinate-system assertions.

Five blanks are inscribed, convex triangulations of ellipsoids. Their actual faceted mesh is the authoritative object for final verification. The smoother analytic ellipsoid is only a search surrogate. One blank has an excluded slab at local x greater than 12 mm.

Perturbations and controls

Local displacement adds a Gaussian Z displacement to both surfaces. Its configured amplitude is the continuous maximum of the field; the maximum over discrete vertices can be slightly smaller. Bending adds a quadratic function of Y to both surfaces. Thinning subtracts a Gaussian field from the top surface only. Reduced relief subtracts a fraction of the relief elevation. The JSON retains the parameter and actual maximum corresponding-vertex displacement when correspondence exists.

Vertical Gaussian noise with a recorded seed, removal of a superior surface patch and a lower-resolution reconstruction are separately labelled acquisition/processing artifacts. Their surface differences do not establish real framework defects. A missing patch can affect fewer than 5% of sampled distances, so a near-zero P95 does not imply complete geometry; the open-boundary warning and Hausdorff result remain relevant.

This run examines software response to known changes. It does not estimate sensitivity, specificity or a clinically meaningful defect threshold. Those require a prespecified severity-by-scan-quality experiment, independent cases and regional definitions reviewed by a surgeon.

Registration

Every synthetic candidate receives the same recorded acquisition pose. Its inverse is used for the known-pose control and to initialize point-to-triangle ICP. This deliberately separates arbitrary scan pose from distortion. ICP applies a proper rotation and translation using SVD/Kabsch fitting; scale stays 1 and reflections are excluded. No deformable registration is used.

The report presents both known-pose and best-fit results. The displayed candidate is the best-fit result. ICP is untrimmed, uses 700 area-uniform source samples by default and stops at an RMSE change below 0.000001 mm or 80 iterations. Termination at the iteration limit produces a warning. The local ICP solution is not an exhaustive rigid-registration optimum. Imported meshes use centroid initialization and may require manual coarse orientation first.

Surface distances

Each direction samples 2,000 points uniformly by triangle area using a fixed pseudorandom seed. Each point is compared to the closest point on any triangle of the opposite mesh. This avoids the bias of nearest-vertex distances on meshes of unequal density.

RMSE is the square root of the mean squared distance over the pooled two-direction samples, with equal weight per direction. P95 is the 95th percentile of that same pool. These are sampling estimates. The report also retains each direction separately.

Regional values are reference-to-candidate distances only: superior Y > 12 mm, central |Y| ≤ 12 mm, inferior Y < −12 mm. They are demonstration partitions, not validated surgical subunits.

The sampled symmetric Hausdorff lower bound includes all original mesh vertices plus area samples in both directions. It must not be called the exact continuous Hausdorff distance. The upper bound uses the 1-Lipschitz property of distance to a surface: the distance anywhere in a source triangle is at most its maximum vertex distance plus its longest edge. The implementation conservatively adds the source mesh's longest edge to each directional maximum vertex distance. The result is intentionally coarse. Adaptive triangle subdivision can tighten it in later work.

Distances to triangles are computed by trimesh proximity queries. Analytic box and parallel-plane tests check the numerical implementation independently of synthetic ear geometry.

Thickness and volume

Construction-column thickness is the top-minus-bottom Z coordinate at paired XY locations in the original construction frame. It has a precise definition for these height-field solids. It is not a general minimum wall-normal thickness or a tissue-strength measure. Maps are carried with the corresponding vertices through rigid alignment. For scan noise, missing surfaces and arbitrary imported geometry this measure is withheld. For bending, equal top/bottom Z displacement preserves the column thickness, but may change thickness measured along surface normals.

Mesh volume is the signed closed-surface volume, accepted only for closed, consistently wound, nondegenerate, positive-volume meshes. The reference volume is independently checked against projected triangle area × average height, summed over the top surface.

Overlap uses 4,096 scrambled Sobol samples in the combined bounding box. Mesh ray queries classify inclusion in each object. Dice and IoU use these common occupancy samples. The report includes the change from half the sample count, which is a resolution sensitivity measure, not a confidence interval. Open surfaces are rejected. Arbitrary closed input meshes can still self-intersect: overlap on imported data additionally requires explicit solid review.

Placement and certificate

All framework dimensions remain fixed. The three methods are:

  1. Fixed orientation: preserve the reference axes and center its axis-aligned bounding box at the blank origin.
  2. Bounding-box alignment: map the framework's sorted world-axis bounding-box extents onto the blank's sorted world-axis extents, using a proper axis permutation. This baseline permits 90-degree axis changes, not a continuously oriented bounding box or anatomical landmark fitting.
  3. Optimized search: seeded, bounded six-degree-of-freedom differential evolution, followed by bounded Powell refinement. The search also retains the baselines and the blank-axis initialization. It selects the candidate with the best independently verified clearance, preferring feasible candidates.

The analytic objective penalizes the largest normalized ellipsoidal radius and any excluded-slab intrusion. It is a surrogate, not the certificate. The global iteration budget and random seed are recorded. A feasible result may occur before the optimizer meets its convergence criterion. Neither optimizer convergence nor failure proves global optimality or impossibility.

The separate verifier computes normalized supporting planes from the blank's convex hull. Every transformed framework vertex must lie at least the required margin plus a 0.000001 mm numerical guard inside every plane. An optional slab is checked as an additional linear half-space. Because the allowed volume is convex, satisfying these inequalities at every vertex also contains every triangle and the full enclosed solid. This argument does not work for a nonconvex anatomical blank.

The verifier rejects scaling, reflections, nonrigid transforms, invalid framework solids, unavailable/insufficient column thickness, and boundary or exclusion violations. For interior points, the minimum supporting-plane slack is the boundary clearance. Negative values outside are constraint violations; they are not claimed to equal Euclidean outside distance.

The 0.8 mm margin and 2.0 mm required column thickness are illustrative parameters. General wall-thickness preservation and tool access are not assessed. Volume ratio is calculated against the full blank and does not change with rigid pose. Excluded-slab volume is not subtracted.

Reproducibility and later study analysis

The configuration, dependency lock, random seed, environment versions, source/acquired/aligned meshes, CSVs, structured JSON and SHA-256 hashes are retained. A scientific-output fingerprint excludes runtime, date, environment and exported-file hashes. Reproduction with identical numerical dependencies is checked separately; other hardware and numerical-library versions may introduce small differences.

Synthetic blank configurations are not independent individuals. No participant-level confidence intervals are presented for the demo. A separate CSV analysis command rejects individual leakage across development/evaluation splits and unmatched observation sets across methods. It resamples entire individuals, including every repeated pair and every method, to form paired percentile-bootstrap intervals for the pair-weighted feasible-placement proportion. It keeps failed attempts in the denominator. The method assumes input rows have already been verified and correctly identified.

Unimplemented research stages

Anatomical NIfTI ingestion, voxel-to-physical transforms, segmentation uncertainty, nonconvex donor containment, general thickness, exact or tighter Hausdorff computation, tool access, modular allocation, a locked 20–30-person pilot, surgeon evaluation and physical tissue experiments are not completed by this demonstration. The next development stages are recorded in docs/roadmap.md.

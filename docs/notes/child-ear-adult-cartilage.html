Pediatric ear target with adult CCSeg cartilage

17 September 2026. Recipient ear size and cartilage-source age are separate design inputs. This development experiment pairs a pediatric clinical-cohort ear benchmark with the author's adult CCSeg group and retains the previous adult-height target as a control on the same anatomy.

Recipient target

The international microtia consensus describes autologous rib-cartilage reconstruction from at least six years, ideally nine or older, with timing dependent on technique and cartilage development. This is guidance, not evidence of the most frequent procedure age. Cadaveric-stock workflows need not inherit every timing constraint imposed by the recipient's own rib development.

Sun et al. (2022) measured unaffected ears in 100 unilateral microtia patients. Their reported mean ear length is 58.88 mm (SD 4.77), width 33.15 mm, and mean age 9.75 years. We use 58.9 mm full external-ear height as a declared pediatric clinical-cohort benchmark. There is an unresolved reporting inconsistency: the methods specify ages 5–10, while the results and table give age 9.75 ± 4.2. Therefore this is not an exact age-nine norm, a universal normal size, or a measured contralateral ear for any CCSeg case.

The same closed external-ear surface is uniformly scaled, preserving every original ear face and its proportions. In-plane component outlines follow this scale; component depths and the helix ribbon width remain fixed engineering parameters. The external surface includes the lobule and soft tissue; it is not native auricular cartilage segmentation. Width is reported rather than independently forced to the study mean.

61.5 mm remains a control, based on the previous young-adult anthropometric benchmark. Both sizes use exactly the same ten adult scans, constraint values, method families and random-seed scheme. Relative to 61.5 mm, 58.9 mm is 4.23% shorter. A smaller height does not establish that the unreviewed component design is anatomically correct.

The clinical workflow associated with our source STL describes an 11-year-old patient. It was inaccurate to characterize the source as an adult ear based on our imposed adult-size normalization. Native STL physical units remain unconfirmed; original patient size is not recovered by this benchmark.

The CCSeg paper's cohort table distinguishes:

We select the ten files under Test_Folder/G3/labelcol/ in the pinned author-distributed Hugging Face repository, revision 9c288ea4585c820de202ebd7838f13072f267f1e. Selection is based on folder/group membership and the paper's definition, not inferred ages encoded in filenames. Exact ages are left null. Selection is recorded before search, and each original compressed file is hashed.

The case IDs are T168-25M, T251-27F, T254-20F, T283-36M, T344-29M, T364-19M, T500-44F, T556-26M, T625-34M and T681-18M-A. Both sides count as one case source. Distinct-file hashes prevent duplicate scans from creating more stock; distinct-person linkage is still not independently verified.

These are living-patient CT anatomical proxies. G3 establishes an adult category; it does not establish suitability or representativeness for older cadaveric tissue-bank donors. The distribution of donor ages, calcification, processing effects and usable tissue still requires appropriate specimen data. The dataset card's age summary and age-like filename tokens are not sufficient to assert an exact oldest participant age.

The authors' segmentation test split is being repurposed for exploratory geometric development. These cases cannot subsequently be called an untouched evaluation cohort for this project.

Intake and interpretation

Original mm units, physical affines and cartilage dimensions are preserved. Each selected label is checked for scan-boundary contact and exported bounds against native voxel bounds. Placement uses the original labeled voxel solids, including gaps, with the existing 0.8 mm margin and 0.8 mm same-stock spacing. These remain engineering assumptions.

The whole-cage centroid heuristic flags left ribs 9/10 in T254-20F: the tenth-label centroid is 1.51 mm superior to the ninth. Centroid order alone is not anatomical numbering proof. We retain this warning; rib 10 is not used for placement. All cases pass left/right separation and the superior ordering checks within the selected 5–9 labels. No labels are renamed, repaired or removed. All rib names remain provisional pending anatomical review.

The same fixed draft components are searched at both heights. The curved helix and straight-stock alternative remain separate architectures; the latter does not validate bending. A search failure is specific to these shapes, search settings and conservative verification, not proof that a surgeon could not construct an ear.

The straight-stock helix alternative also remains at 2 / 4 for both sizes. Neither base nor helix has a verified candidate under the tested shapes and constraints. At 58.9 mm, one selected solution uses T344-29M's left 6+7 region for the antihelix and right 6+7 region for the tragus, with conservative clearance bounds of 0.801 and 0.842 mm. Both sides count as one case source.

The earlier unfiltered ten-case experiment allocated only the tragus. Its different cohort prevents attributing the improvement to a single cause. In the new paired experiment the antihelix fits at both heights, so shrinking the target alone does not explain the improvement. Construction geometry, margin assumptions and finite search remain unresolved contributors; these results do not demonstrate clinical impossibility.

Independent verification passed: 7,686 candidate rigid transforms checked, 201 accepted real-anatomy candidates recertified, and 180 allocation records replayed. All 100 exported cartilage surfaces agree with native voxel bounds within 0.000029 mm (numerical export consistency, not anatomical accuracy). Both ear sizes, in STL and PLY formats, preserve all 63,204 faces, form one watertight body and recover the intended scaled source geometry. The software suite passes 51 tests.

Reproduce

Download only the adult group's public segmentation files:

.venv/bin/hf download haifan-gong/CCSeg --type dataset \
  --revision 9c288ea4585c820de202ebd7838f13072f267f1e \
  --include 'Test_Folder/G3/labelcol/*.nii.gz' \
  --local-dir data/public_anatomical/CCSeg/raw --max-workers 3
.venv/bin/python scripts/rebuild_child_adult.py
.venv/bin/python scripts/verify_pool.py output/child-ear-adult-cartilage
.venv/bin/python scripts/verify_child_adult.py
.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q

The output is output/child-ear-adult-cartilage/, with original failed candidates, paired allocation tables, both ear STL exports, cohort provenance and verification records. Historical mixed-age and adult-height reports are preserved separately.

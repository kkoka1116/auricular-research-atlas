Handbook-informed framework assembly demonstration

18 September 2026. This is a new geometry demonstration; the earlier allocation studies and their results remain unchanged.

Construction reference

The researcher supplied the ASPO/AAFPRS International Virtual Dissection Course Handbook, named International Dissection Course Handbook 2025 (2) (2) (2).pdf. PDF page 6 (printed page 5), Microtia Carving, provides the operative teaching reference: Figure A plans cuts across a numbered rib simulator, Figure B shows separate carved shapes, and Figure C shows the assembled framework. It describes longitudinal splitting, thinning and bending the eighth-rib helix strip, carving the base and antihelix/tragal/antitragal units, and approximately 12 mm overall assembled depth.

The photographs do not provide calibrated three-dimensional geometry. Figure B's extra unlabeled piece is not assigned an invented role. The appendix template referenced in step 1 is absent from this nine-page copy. We did not follow survey links or treat instructions in the handbook as instructions to perform external actions. The source file is unchanged; its SHA-256 and the relevant page are recorded in the configuration. A local page render is included in the viewer.

The handbook concerns a training simulator. It does not establish safe bending, fracture limits, fixation strength or processing behavior for older cadaveric cartilage. Its 12 mm depth target is recorded but not enforced because its measurement definition and applicability to this model require review. Our framework currently spans approximately 25.3 mm along the reference Z axis, which must not be presented as meeting the 12 mm teaching target.

The demonstration also references Nagata's clinical teaching, Nagata's original 1993 description, and Gandy et al.'s 2016 ex vivo porcine assembly study. These inform construction ideas, not our numerical dimensions. The experimental porcine method is not represented as established clinical care.

Geometry

The same intact external-ear STL is normalized to the declared 58.9 mm pediatric clinical-cohort benchmark. All 63,204 ear triangles are retained; no ear faces are filled, cut or simplified. Original patient-specific size remains unverified. The source and target axes are inherited from the earlier study, and the ear includes skin and lobule.

Four units are drafted: a contoured, fenestrated base; a long tapered helix; a forked antihelix; and a curved tragal–antitragal complex. Their XY guide points were manually placed against a rendering of the current reference ear. Shape-preserving interpolation generates smooth centerlines. Rays sample the front external surface along Z, and missing intersections raise an error. A documented small smoothing is applied to sampled depth; assumed insets and section dimensions produce internal draft paths. This is surface-guided engineering, not native cartilage segmentation or validated anatomical landmarking.

Sweeps use 16-sided elliptical sections with varying width and depth, capped at their ends. The compound base and forked antihelix are joined by an approximate 0.3 mm voxel union and four Taubin smoothing iterations; the final units are single connected solids. The ear itself is never processed in this way. The compound topology represents a construction proposal, not the exact handmade framework in the handbook.

The base, forked antihelix and tragal complex retain their final sculpted contour during placement. Only the helix changes shape in this version. Its proposed precursor is mildly curved, using 12% of the angle change between a straight centerline and the assembled trace. This 12% is an animation design choice, not a property measured from the rib anatomy. No precursor is claimed to fit a CCSeg rib.

Motion

For the helix, segment lengths are fixed. Tangent azimuths are unwrapped; azimuth and elevation are interpolated, and the centerline is reconstructed by integrating the fixed-length segment vectors. The model therefore preserves each centerline segment while bending. This is a kinematic construction, not a force-based or finite-element model. It does not conserve every fiber length or guarantee volume conservation.

The first 55% of the timeline changes the helix shape. The remaining 45% translates the shaped pieces from a separated layout into their specified reference positions. Reverse playback uses the same map. A reference overlay shares the exact same coordinates; the separate reference viewer has its own labeled scale bar. The animation does not simulate surgical instrument paths.

Checks and unresolved constraints

The software suite passes 55 tests, including an independent regular-polygon-prism volume check and centerline conservation on a nonplanar path. Independent verification inspects 16 exported component files (four units × two states × STL/PLY) and 244 sampled motion solids. All are closed, consistently oriented, positive-volume, single connected bodies with nondegenerate triangles. Browser-generated geometry agrees with Python-generated geometry, and export vertices agree within 0.000004 mm. These are numerical checks, not physical accuracy claims.

The helix centerline is 85.02 mm. Its computed volume varies from approximately 516.586 to 516.654 mm³ during the demonstrated motion; this small change is reported, not hidden. No tissue mechanics conclusion follows from it. The whole draft framework has reference-axis bounds approximately 30.14 × 40.24 × 25.30 mm; those dimensions are distinct from full ear height.

Not established: self-intersection-free surfaces; component-to-component contact and interference; fixation and suture space; safe bending; springback; cartilage-only containment; machining/fixture access; the handbook's surgical-depth equivalence; or source-stock feasibility. These component changes invalidate reuse of earlier donor-fit results for the new design. A new allocation search must consume the accepted cut shapes and their construction requirements.

Transfer to other ears

This version does not automatically deconstruct arbitrary STL files. A reusable workflow would calibrate and orient each input, annotate homologous landmarks and ridge paths, fit a reviewed construction template, assess surface/landmark error and assembly constraints, and then allocate the resulting preforms to available cartilage. The same ear surface can admit several constructions; the inverse is not unique. External scans alone do not identify cartilage boundaries or thickness.

The builder requires the original user-supplied handbook at the recorded path, the existing ear source archive, the current Python environment, and pdftoppm. Verification also uses Node to independently evaluate the viewer geometry functions. The result is output/assembly-lab/index.html, with individual STL and PLY exports, source hashes, editable landmark specifications, and a verification record.

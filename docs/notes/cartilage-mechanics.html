Cartilage Mechanics Lab

Research implementation, 18 September 2026. Local viewer: output/mechanics-lab/index.html.

What this adds

A three-dimensional mechanics calculation now connects a proposed curved strip in adult costal-cartilage anatomy with the outer rim of the 58.9 mm ear reference. The helix bends and twists under idealized holding fixtures. The other three draft components remain rigid, already-carved shapes, associated with the source regions described in the handbook and surgical teaching.

This is a parameterized feasibility model, not an identification of the mechanical properties of a CCSeg patient. It predicts conditional equilibrium shapes and forces, not fracture, biological durability, permanent reshaping or clinical suitability.

Anatomy and construction

The preserved source is the G3 adult CCSeg case T500-44F, label 18, provisionally interpreted as right eighth cartilage. G3 membership establishes the dataset's adult category; exact age is unknown and was not parsed from the filename. These are living-patient CT segmentations. They do not establish older cadaveric tissue properties. The case was selected after an exploratory clearance screen of the 20 eighth-cartilage labels in the ten available adult cases. It is a favorable development example, not a random sample or a success-rate estimate.

The original coded NIfTI affine, explicit millimeter units and file hash are checked. The native masks are unchanged. The centerline is a geometric estimate: PCA-coordinate bins weighted toward interior voxels, smoothing, and cubic interpolation. A window is selected along that curve to maximize a conservative clearance bound. Its endpoints are proposed cut landmarks, not segmented costochondral junctions. Endpoints, native-to-ear rigid transform, full trace and processing settings are saved in the report.

The original traced ear-rim centerline is 85.0234 mm. Resampling to 48 rod segments gives 84.9570 mm, a 0.0664 mm decrease; the full ear surface remains 58.9 mm high with all 63,204 triangles. Source points are selected to match those edge lengths without scaling the cartilage or ear. Constant elliptical sections of 1.5, 2.4 or 3.4 mm rim width by 2.7 mm depth replace the earlier tapered draft helix for a controlled section-size comparison. These are explicit design assumptions, not measured auricular cartilage thicknesses.

Your handbook's Microtia Carving page (PDF page 6, printed page 5) identifies the numbered simulator ribs and the thinning/bending sequence for the helix. Nagata's clinical teaching associates sixth/seventh cartilage with the base, eighth with the helix, ninth with the antihelix and a remaining suitable portion with the tragal unit. We highlight those regions on the native segmentation. A nearest-surface pair locates a proposed sixth/seventh interface; this does not establish a fused synchondrosis. Other components have no new cut placement or allocation certificate. The handbook's approximately 12 mm overall framework depth is still unresolved for this draft geometry.

Material evidence and assumptions

Grellmann et al. (2006) report a human costal-cartilage flexural modulus of 8.8 ± 2.9 MPa. We use 8.8 MPa as one linear approximation and 5.9/11.7 MPa as sensitivity scenarios. These are not confidence limits, a population distribution or a donor calibration.

A separate nonlinear comparison uses loading coefficients reported by Goh and Anderson (2025; online 2024): E₁ = 34.0 MPa and E₃ = 17.0 GPa, taken from the Results section. They tested intact ribs 3–6 from 24 donors aged 47–94, with perichondrium retained. The two medians are combined illustratively; they are not a paired measured specimen. Their transfer to a carved eighth-rib strip is unvalidated. The paper demonstrates substantial variability and nonlinear loading/unloading behavior. Its roughly 5% nominal test-strain scale supplies an extrapolation flag, not a fracture or safety limit.

Weber et al. (2021) establish that direction and test method affect measured properties. We therefore do not treat indentation or compression moduli as interchangeable bending calibrations. Lau et al. (2008) demonstrate load relaxation; a transferable time law for this carved strip was not obtained. There is no tissue recovery clock in the model. No inference that an older donor is necessarily stiffer is made.

Other assumptions: homogeneous material, symmetric tensile/compressive law, elliptical cross-sections, Poisson ratio 0.45, and a stress-free carved strip retaining the traced source curve. Perichondrium, spatial calcification, material anisotropy and cutting-induced residual stress are not modeled.

Mechanics

The solver is a discrete, finite-rotation Kirchhoff rod. Each segment carries an orthonormal material frame. Integrating its tangent with a fixed segment length enforces an inextensible, unshearable centerline. Relative-frame rotation logarithms give curvature and twist; the corresponding intrinsic rest values are subtracted. Global rigid rotation leaves strain energy unchanged.

For section semiaxes a and b, area A = πab and curvature change Δκ, define q² = (b Δκ₁)² + (a Δκ₂)². The first-order outer-fiber bending-strain estimate is q. The symmetric axial law is σ = E₁ε + E₃ε³. Integrating it over the elliptical section gives bending energy per length:

E₁ A q² / 8 + E₃ A q⁴ / 32.

Torsional energy per length is G J Δκ₃² / 2, with G = E₁ / [2(1+ν)] and the Saint-Venant ellipse torsion constant J = πa³b³/(a²+b²). This torsion law is an isotropic approximation, not a measured torsional response. The first-order strain description may become inaccurate at high strain or high intrinsic curvature.

Twelve isotropic positional springs, each 1 N/mm, attach points to a moving guide; the first segment is clamped. Guide positions follow an inextensible rotation interpolation toward the template. The displayed strip is the independently optimized equilibrium, not the guide interpolation. Fixtures may push or pull in three dimensions; they are not tension-only sutures, measured stitch stiffnesses or verified surgical stitch positions. The carved base does not carry their calculated reaction forces.

Nine forming states and five fixture-release states are computed per scenario. Release reduces fixture stiffness while leaving the guide at the final ear target. At zero stiffness the elastic model has a known zero-energy rest solution. This is equilibrium springback with a clamped root, not a dynamic recovery trajectory or a prediction of permanent set. Local optimization is used; a global minimum is not proven.

All dimensions are millimeters, force is Newtons, moduli are MPa = N/mm², and energy is N·mm. The metric shown as force is the largest single fixture force, not the total hand force or a suture failure load.

Containment and verification

Containment covers the complete undeformed strip using conservative capsules around every polygonal segment. A subdivided midpoint-ball cover includes the largest cross-section radius; native-voxel distance bounds include voxel half-diagonal and off-center corrections. No convex hull or rescaled/smoothed mask is used. The 1.5 and 2.4 mm options have a reported lower-bound clearance of 1.0337 mm, exceeding the retained 0.8 mm margin. The 3.4 mm option yields only 0.6837 mm and is not certified; this is not proof it cannot fit.

Independent checks use a nearest-background-voxel-center tree rather than the search certificate's distance-field transfer. The verification report also records closed exported solids, native-coordinate round trips, exact segment-length preservation, force-equilibrium gradients, JavaScript/Python geometry agreement and the unchanged reference ear. Meaningful solver tests cover energy-gradient agreement beyond canonical rotation angles, stress-free intrinsic curvature, rotational invariance, analytical bending energy and cantilever convergence. A 48-to-96 segment check for the default material/section gives 0.0099 mm RMS shape difference; peak fixture force changes from 0.122 to 0.130 N and peak strain from 9.29% to 9.38%. This is not a convergence bound for every scenario. All 168 states pass the equilibrium check; all 39 exported solids are closed. The full test suite passes 61 tests.

The default held model estimates approximately 0.13 mm centerline RMS error, 0.122 N peak fixture force and 9.3% peak bending strain. The strain exceeds the comparison test scale, and these values must not be treated as validated tissue predictions. Inspect verification.json for numerical errors and refinement results. A coarse self-contact screen is included; exact self-intersection, inter-component contact, tool access and machining feasibility are not established.

Reproduce and validate physically

Run scripts/rebuild_mechanics.py, scripts/verify_mechanics.py and the test suite with the project Python environment. Material values, section sizes, source identity and fixture assumptions are in config/mechanics.json. The local report includes meshes, solved states, parameters and source hashes; earlier reports remain unchanged.

For specimen calibration, measure the free shape after carving, dimensions, condition/storage and any calcification. Perform controlled bend-and-release tests on strips with relevant rib level, section and preparation. Record force, displacement and recovery over time; image curvature and track damage rather than assigning an unmeasured safe-strain threshold. Fit material and time-response parameters on development specimens, then compare predicted shapes and forces with separate specimens. Surgical review should establish component geometry, the depth convention, cut landmarks and attachment behavior before expanding to a coupled full-framework model.

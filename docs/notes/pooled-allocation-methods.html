Pooled allocation development experiment

Ten CCSeg training cases, selected by filename order from the pinned manifest, serve as provisional specimen proxies. The original three cases were already inspected; seven additional cases were selected before searching their geometry. Dataset case IDs are not confirmed unique individuals or available cadaveric grafts. Both sides of one case count as one source. These are development data, not a locked evaluation set.

The allocator considers the same four draft pieces using one case, then at most 2, 3, 4 or 10 case sources. Its objective maximizes component coverage, then minimizes sources, then maximizes summed certified clearance. A larger pool cannot supply a component with no certified candidate anywhere in the catalog. Search failure does not prove continuous placement impossible.

Each candidate retains case, specimen group, coordinate-frame hash, side, original integer labels, component, transform and certificate. Distinct specimens have separate physical coordinates. Same-stock nonoverlap is certified using disjoint labeled domains with inward margins or separating axes of enclosing boxes. Duplicate acquisitions cannot become extra stock by changing names. Unregistered acquisitions of the same specimen cannot be jointly consumed.

The original search settings are retained: 0.8 mm inward margin, 0.8 mm component spacing, seeded rigid searches, fixed-orientation and PCA baselines. These are engineering assumptions. Ribs 5, 6+7, 8 and 9 on both sides are provisional interpretations of numeric labels. No label gaps are filled and no tissue quality or tool access is inferred.

All individually accepted candidates undergo fresh complete-solid containment checks and independent distances from sample points to background voxel cuboids. Allocations retain unallocated pieces and the reason. A finite catalog-search node limit is explicit. Even an exhaustive catalog search is not exhaustive over all possible placements.

Original size experiment

The original 55, 60 and 65 mm targets describe distances between two draft projected landmarks, not full ear heights. The report output/pool/ preserves those definitions. It contains 1,200 region-component search problems: 120 reused historical searches at 60 mm and 1,080 additional searches. Reuse requires matching code, configuration, report fingerprint and mask hashes.

Across all sizes, 11,917 candidate records are retained, including failures; 85 are individually certified. These are alternative poses, not independent donors or implants. At all three targets the catalog supplied only the tragus, 1/4 required components, including when multiple cases were allowed. The 270 allocation records cover cases, methods, architectures and source budgets. An independent rerun recomputed the 1,080 additional searches and matched the scientific fingerprint:

Revised intact reference

Following the face-loss audit, output/components-population/ and output/pool-population/ use connected-body ear extraction and a literature-based 61.5 mm full ear height. This is a new geometry version. See ear extraction and sizing. The old 55/60/65 landmark scenarios must not be relabeled as full ear heights. The revised 61.5 mm run retains 3,967 candidate poses, with 28 individually certified tragus poses and 90 allocation records. It again supplies only 1/4 pieces from the real case pool; no complete framework allocation is found. All 28 accepted poses were recertified against native masks and all 90 allocations were replayed.

The earlier en-bloc experiment also used a different synthetic framework and is not a matched comparator for the four draft parts.

Synthetic allocation control

Four purpose-built rectangular blocks supply a curated catalog: source A offers base and tragus; source B offers helix and antihelix. One source covers 2/4 pieces and two sources cover 4/4. Every solid placement is certified, and same-source pieces are separated. Other block-component combinations were not searched; the example tests catalog allocation and does not prove two donors would be necessary in an unrestricted geometric search. It is not cartilage anatomy.

Clinical and data limits

Sharma et al. (2025) reports preliminary allogeneic costal-cartilage reconstruction. It does not establish that mixing multiple donors within a framework is safe or equivalent. This software supplies no biological, processing, assembly or machining validation.

CCSeg's conflicting license declarations remain unresolved; original masks and derivatives stay local. Ear-derived geometry is attributed under CC BY-SA 4.0. No available tissue-bank inventory, calcification annotation, viability assessment or surgically reviewed exclusion zones are supplied.

Reports include model hashes, original masks, source hashes, constraints and failure records. The verifier reloads native masks, checks scan spacing against affine scale and full-resolution mesh extents, checks rigid transforms, recertifies accepted candidates and replays allocations. These unit checks do not establish scanner calibration, segmentation accuracy or the appropriate patient target size.

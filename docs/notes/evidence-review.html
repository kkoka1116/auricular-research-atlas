Surgical evidence and its implementation

Research update: 17 September 2026. Krishna Koka's supplied Ritter_Research_Brief.pdf (11 September 2026) was read in full, including its four rendered pages. This is a focused engineering review, not a systematic review or clinical practice guideline. Published abstracts were used where full text was unavailable; that distinction is retained below. No article figures were reused.

What the existing brief establishes

The broader project is patient-specific CAD/CAM fabrication from allogeneic costal cartilage. The proposed physical experiment compares manual, template-guided and CNC fabrication within donor blocks, with a 60-minute geometric endpoint, earlier timed scans, mechanical tests and documented tissue processing. Its proposed 12-donor physical pilot is separate from the earlier 20–30-person computational anatomy pilot. Neither study has been executed by this software.

The current CCSeg step addresses anatomy and placement-method development. It cannot supply tissue-bank lot records, processing history, freezing effects, machining access, fixture deformation, viability or postoperative outcomes. The old brief's hypotheses and proposed thresholds remain proposals, not observed findings.

Decisions implemented now

  1. Import three training scans, selected before inspection, and one corresponding CT. Retain original files and hashes at a pinned repository revision. No validation/test scan is used for development.
  2. Preserve all 20 integer labels per scan. Infer candidate left 1–10 and right 1–10 from their RAS arrangement and descending anatomical order; retain that inference as unverified. The paper names anatomical segments but does not provide a verified integer lookup in the material retrieved.
  3. Expose individual labels and candidate harvest-region selections in a native-dimension viewer. Use unsmoothed native-grid geometry for computations and separate coarser surfaces for rendering.
  4. Compare fixed anatomical orientation, PCA box alignment and a seeded rigid search, keeping the reference dimensions fixed. Evaluate candidate sixth/seventh unions and individual eighth/ninth regions bilaterally. The union preserves every gap and does not assert that adjacent pieces can be harvested together.
  5. Verify the entire solid against the nonconvex voxel domain, with explicit conservative bounds. Use a tiny cube as an independently checked software control; it is not a proposed ear component.
  6. Recheck selected poses at additional inward boundary margins. These scenarios are not calibrated estimates of annotation uncertainty.

Parameters that still require expert definition

The default 0.8 mm clearance and 2.0 mm construction-column thickness are illustrative engineering settings inherited from the synthetic demo. The retrieved papers do not establish them as universal surgical thresholds. Real minimum tissue thickness, useful stock boundaries, calcification/exclusion grading, component interfaces, fixture allowances and tool access require a reviewed protocol. No clinical-age rule, donor selection rule or automatic harvest recommendation is implemented.

Dr. Ritter's next review should confirm cartilage identities and junction continuity, supply/approve the framework, choose an architecture, and define the measurements and constraints that would make a fit result meaningful. Until then, this output is an anatomical development demonstration, not the locked pilot.
